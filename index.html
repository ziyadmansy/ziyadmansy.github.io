About

My goal is automated testing that catches bugs and security vulnerabilities before attackers exploit them or users pay for them, especially silent failures that never crash, and gives developers a short list of real defects to fix instead of a flood of false alarms. My current work asks how LLM-based testing agents acquire the domain knowledge they need, how to build test oracles for software without reliable crash signals, and how differential testing can expose behavior that no single implementation reveals.

In 2026 I turned a research challenge into three sole-authored studies on LLM-guided fuzzing, all now under review, while working full-time as a senior engineer. Along the way my findings led Google-maintained libraries to document behavior that silently changed data, including a documentation fix of mine that was merged into json_serializable.

Before research, I spent five years shipping Android, iOS and Flutter apps in regulated domains: a payments platform licensed by the Saudi Central Bank, where I led the team that closed every penetration-test finding with zero production security incidents, and HIPAA-compliant transportation apps handling thousands of trips a day in the US. Research

My research started with a fuzzing challenge that Dr. Marcelo D'Amorim (NC State) set for prospective Ph.D. applicants; my solution passed and I was one of about 20 of 70 applicants invited to a Q&A session. Each study since has answered the question the previous one left open:

  • Can an LLM improve a fuzzer without seeing inside the target? Yes on cJSON (58.2% → 97.1% valid inputs), but a pre-registered replication on a second parser reversed the effect, and I traced every rejected input to its cause.
  • What happens when there is no crash to detect? A differential oracle across four Dart deserializers showed the LLM loop lagged a static generator 2.9% vs. 22.2%; four facts of domain knowledge closed the gap entirely.
  • Can the fuzzer acquire that knowledge itself? A pre-registered study across Dart and Kotlin found self-acquired knowledge helps where interesting behavior is dense (50% → 67%, 82% with systematic probing) and misses it where it is narrow.

All code, data and per-run artifacts are public, with designs committed before any data and every deviation logged.
